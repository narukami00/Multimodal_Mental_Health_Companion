Literature Review & Requirement Analysis
Multimodal (Facial, Voice, Text) Emotion Recognition for
AI-Based Mental Health Emotion Monitoring
Prepared in support of the project proposal:
"AI-Based Mental Health Emotion Monitoring from Facial and Voice Features Using Multimodal Artificial Intelligence"
0. Scope, Method, and How to Use This Review
This review was compiled by searching across top-tier venues in affective computing, computer vision, speech processing, NLP, and multimodal machine learning: NeurIPS, CVPR/CVPRW, ICCV, ACL/EMNLP/NAACL, Interspeech/ICASSP, IEEE Transactions on Affective Computing (TAFFC), Information Fusion, Pattern Recognition, Expert Systems with Applications, and the ABAW (Affective Behavior Analysis in-the-Wild) and MER (Multimodal Emotion Recognition) workshop challenge series. Since your project's own scope spans facial expression analysis, speech emotion recognition, and their fusion, this review is organized the same way: facial-only work, speech-only work, text/conversational work, fully multimodal fusion architectures, LLM-based/generalist approaches, and finally work specific to your actual target domain — mental health and depression/distress detection from these same signals. Priority was given to papers combining video/face with audio, per your instruction, while single-modality (audio-only or text-only) papers were kept where they represent foundational or SOTA building blocks you will likely reuse as encoders inside a fused pipeline.
Each entry lists: the problem being solved, the exact input/output contract, the core methodology, and — most important for your requirement analysis — explicit limitations and gaps. Section 8 converts these gaps into a direct gap analysis against your proposal, and Section 9 gives requirement-analysis takeaways (functional and non-functional requirements implied by the literature).
1. Foundational & Landscape Surveys (Read These First)
These give you the taxonomy of the entire field — fusion strategies (early/late/hybrid/attention-based), dataset landscape, and open problems. Use them as your Chapter 2 backbone.
A Survey of Deep Learning-Based Multimodal Emotion Recognition: Speech, Text, and Face
Entropy (MDPI), 2023   |   Q1 Journal
Link: https://www.mdpi.com/1099-4300/25/10/1440
Problem: Lack of a focused, deep-learning-specific review of MER combining speech, text, and face — prior surveys were either too broad (all affective computing) or outdated (pre-deep-learning).
Input / Output: Not a model — a meta-analysis. Reviews datasets (input types: audio waveforms, video frames, transcripts) and the fusion-to-emotion-label output pipelines of ~80+ papers.
Methodology: Systematic taxonomy of (a) unimodal feature extraction methods per modality, (b) fusion methods — feature-level (early), decision-level (late), and model-level (hybrid/attention), and (c) evaluation protocols.
Limitations / Gaps identified: Notes persistent gaps in: cross-corpus generalization (models overfit to one dataset's recording conditions), poor handling of missing/noisy modalities, and a near-total lack of real-world (as opposed to acted/lab) evaluation data.
Multimodal Emotion Recognition with Deep Learning: Advancements, Challenges, and Future Directions
Information Fusion, Vol. 105, 2024 (Elsevier)   |   Q1 Journal — high-impact (IF ~18)
Link: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.inffus.2023.102218
Problem: Synthesizes how multimodal fusion of speech, text, facial expression, and physiological signals improves on unimodal emotion recognition, and where deep architectures still fail.
Input / Output: Survey; catalogs input modalities (face/video, speech, text, EEG/physiological) mapped to categorical (Ekman's 6) or dimensional (valence-arousal) emotion outputs.
Methodology: Structured comparison of CNN/RNN/Transformer/GNN-based fusion methods; explicit discussion of attention-based and graph-based fusion as the emerging dominant paradigm over simple concatenation.
Limitations / Gaps identified: Flags (1) the modality heterogeneity gap between continuous signals (audio/video) and discrete text, (2) lack of standard benchmarks for real-world/in-the-wild robustness, (3) minimal explainability in fusion decisions — directly relevant to your 'why' requirement in the emotion output.
A Survey on Multimodal Emotion Recognition: Methods, Datasets, and Future Directions
Computers, Materials & Continua (CMC / Tech Science Press), 2025   |   Q1/Q2 Journal (recent, covers 2021–2025)
Link: https://www.techscience.com/cmc/v87n2/66647/html
Problem: Most existing surveys are already stale given the 2024–2025 shift toward foundation models; this one specifically consolidates the LLM/foundation-model era of MER.
Input / Output: Survey covering visual, acoustic, textual, and sensor-based embeddings and their fusion into emotion predictions.
Methodology: Chronological analysis 2021→2025 emphasizing how pre-trained encoders (CLIP, wav2vec, BERT-family) and self-supervised learning replaced hand-crafted features; also covers graph-reasoning fusion and generative recovery of missing modalities.
Limitations / Gaps identified: Explicitly states the field remains 'fragmented' due to differences in feature alignment, temporal synchronization, modality reliability, and robustness to noisy/missing input — precisely the conditions your real-world deployment (users on random webcams/mics) will face.
2. Facial Expression Recognition (FER) — Vision-Only Backbone
Since your pipeline needs a face-emotion sub-model as one branch of the fusion network, these are the current best-in-class architectures and their exact benchmark numbers (RAF-DB / AffectNet / FERPlus are the three standard leaderboards).
POSTER: A Pyramid Cross-Fusion Transformer Network for Facial Expression Recognition
ICCV Workshops (CVF/IEEE), 2023   |   Top-tier CV conference workshop
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.04083
Problem: Three core FER problems: inter-class similarity (e.g., fear vs. surprise look alike), intra-class discrepancy (same emotion looks different across people), and scale sensitivity (face size/pose variance).
Input / Output: Input: single face image (cropped/aligned). Output: discrete emotion class (7 or 8 classes depending on dataset).
Methodology: A 'pyramid cross-fusion transformer' that fuses two streams — raw image features and facial-landmark features — using cross-attention, plus a multi-scale/pyramid structure to handle scale variance.
Limitations / Gaps identified: Static-image only — no temporal modeling, so it cannot use how an expression evolves over a spoken sentence (a gap directly relevant to your video-based, continuous-speech scenario). Also trained/tested on curated datasets (RAF-DB 92.05%, AffectNet 67.31%, FERPlus 91.62%), which are still closer to posed/semi-wild photos than an uncontrolled webcam feed.
POSTER++: A Simpler and Stronger Facial Expression Recognition Network
Pattern Recognition (Elsevier), 2024   |   Q1 Journal
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12149
Problem: POSTER's own limitation — high parameter count/compute cost — making it impractical for real-time or mobile deployment.
Input / Output: Same as POSTER: single face image → emotion class.
Methodology: Simplifies the cross-fusion design (removes redundant landmark stream stages, uses a lighter vision backbone) while retaining accuracy, explicitly targeting deployability.
Limitations / Gaps identified: Still single-frame/static; still requires a separate face-detection+alignment preprocessing stage; accuracy on 'in-the-wild' compound/ambiguous expressions remains materially below lab-condition accuracy.
Spatio-Temporal Transformer for Dynamic Facial Expression Recognition in the Wild
ACM Multimedia (ACM MM), 2022   |   Top-tier multimedia conference
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.04749
Problem: Directly addresses your requirement: FER from video (dynamic facial expression recognition, DFER), not single images — expressions must be tracked across frames/time.
Input / Output: Input: a short video clip (sequence of frames) of a face. Output: single emotion label for the clip.
Methodology: A spatio-temporal transformer applies self-attention both across spatial regions of the face and across the time dimension, learning which frames/moments carry the most emotional signal (analogous to your need to weight moments where the user is actively speaking vs. silent).
Limitations / Gaps identified: Trained on DFEW/AFEW-style short clips with a single dominant emotion label per clip; does not handle continuously varying emotion within one long recording, nor does it fuse audio — you'd need to adapt this class of model to segment-then-classify per utterance, which is exactly the segmentation problem you described (deciding sentence/utterance boundaries from pause gaps).
Deep Facial Expression Recognition: A Survey
IEEE Transactions on Affective Computing (TAFFC), 2020 (still the most-cited FER survey; widely cited by 2024–2025 papers)   |   Q1 Journal
Link: https://doi.org/10.1109/TAFFC.2020.2981446
Problem: Comprehensive taxonomy of deep FER: pipelines, datasets, and the specific 'in-the-wild' challenges (illumination, pose, occlusion, identity bias).
Input / Output: Survey — covers both static-image and dynamic-sequence FER pipelines.
Methodology: Breaks FER into preprocessing (face detection/alignment/normalization), deep feature learning (CNN/RNN/GAN-based), and the two families of methods: static-frame FER vs. dynamic-sequence FER.
Limitations / Gaps identified: Identifies illumination, head-pose variation, and identity/demographic bias as the three dominant real-world failure modes — all directly applicable to a webcam-based application where you cannot control lighting or camera angle.
AffectNet+: A Database for Enhancing Facial Expression Recognition with Soft-Labels
arXiv 2024 (dataset/benchmark paper, submitted to a CV venue)   |   Dataset paper, CV community
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.22506
Problem: Original AffectNet uses single hard labels per image, but real facial expressions are often ambiguous/compound (e.g., simultaneously sad and angry) — hard labels throw away real signal and inflate reported accuracy gaps between models.
Input / Output: Re-annotated AffectNet images with soft/probabilistic labels across emotion categories instead of one-hot labels.
Methodology: Crowd re-annotation protocol producing a distribution over emotions per image; benchmarks how existing SOTA FER models perform when evaluated against soft ground truth.
Limitations / Gaps identified: Confirms that reported 60–70% 'accuracy' numbers on AffectNet are partly an artifact of forcing single-label ground truth; real emotion expression is genuinely multi-label/fuzzy — a design implication for your system's output format (confidence-distribution output, which your proposal already plans, is the right call).
3. Speech Emotion Recognition (SER) — Audio-Only Backbone
Self-supervised speech models (wav2vec 2.0, HuBERT) have replaced hand-crafted MFCC-only pipelines as the SOTA approach since ~2021. Since your proposal lists MFCC/pitch/spectrogram features, note that most current top-venue work now uses these pretrained encoders instead of or in addition to MFCCs.
Exploring Wav2vec 2.0 Fine-Tuning for Improved Speech Emotion Recognition
ICASSP 2023 (IEEE Intl. Conf. on Acoustics, Speech and Signal Processing)   |   Top-tier speech-processing conference
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06309
Problem: How to best fine-tune a large self-supervised speech model (wav2vec 2.0, pretrained on raw audio for ASR) for the very different downstream task of emotion recognition.
Input / Output: Input: raw speech waveform. Output: categorical emotion class (IEMOCAP's 4-class: angry/happy/neutral/sad, a very common SER benchmark subset).
Methodology: Compares vanilla fine-tuning (V-FT) against task-adaptive pretraining (TAPT) and a proposed pseudo-label TAPT (P-TAPT); establishes new SOTA on IEMOCAP.
Limitations / Gaps identified: Sensitive to how much labeled data is available per speaker/session; performance degrades under low-resource fine-tuning; IEMOCAP itself is scripted/semi-acted dialogue, not spontaneous real-world speech — a domain-shift risk for your app's real users.
A Fine-Tuned Wav2vec 2.0/HuBERT Benchmark for Speech Emotion Recognition, Speaker Verification and Spoken Language Understanding
arXiv / IEEE SLT 2022 workshop   |   Widely cited (600+) SER benchmark paper
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.02735
Problem: Establishes whether self-supervised ASR-style pretraining transfers usefully to non-ASR speech tasks, emotion recognition among them.
Input / Output: Raw waveform in; weighted accuracy on IEMOCAP SER out (79.58% speaker-dependent, 73.01% speaker-independent).
Methodology: Full vs. partial fine-tuning of wav2vec 2.0/HuBERT with a lightweight downstream classification head.
Limitations / Gaps identified: Speaker-independent setting (i.e., emotion recognition generalizing to a person the model has never heard) drops accuracy by ~6.5 points from the speaker-dependent setting — a critical gap since your application will meet entirely new users/voices every session.
HCAM: Hierarchical Cross Attention Model for Multi-Modal Emotion Recognition
arXiv 2023 (referenced widely in 2024-25 SER/MER challenge papers, e.g., Interspeech-affiliated work)   |   Strong community adoption
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.06910
Problem: Standard concatenation-based audio+text fusion ignores that emotional cues are unevenly distributed in time — some words/moments carry more emotional weight than others.
Input / Output: Speech + corresponding transcript in; emotion class out (IEMOCAP).
Methodology: A hierarchical attention structure: word-level cross-attention between audio and text first, then utterance-level attention aggregates across the dialogue/session context (i.e., considers previous utterances, not just the current one).
Limitations / Gaps identified: No visual/facial modality at all (bimodal audio+text only); relies on accurate ASR transcription as input — errors in speech-to-text propagate directly into wrong text-embedding cues, a real risk if you add live ASR.
Decoding Emotions: A Comprehensive Multilingual Study of Speech Models for Speech Emotion Recognition
arXiv 2023 (Interspeech-style benchmark study)   |   Comprehensive benchmark, high citation velocity
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08713
Problem: Prior SER benchmark comparisons of self-supervised models were mostly English/single-language; unclear which pretrained speech backbone (wav2vec2, HuBERT, WavLM, Whisper-encoder, etc.) generalizes best.
Input / Output: Raw audio across multiple languages/corpora in; emotion class out.
Methodology: Systematic benchmarking of multiple SSL speech encoders across multiple SER corpora with consistent downstream classifier heads, isolating the encoder as the only variable.
Limitations / Gaps identified: Explicitly notes English-only fits your project scope, which simplifies your model choice, but the paper flags that even 'best' models show large corpus-to-corpus variance — i.e., picking a backbone requires testing on data resembling your actual deployment (spontaneous conversational monologue, not acted lines).
4. Text-Based & Conversational Emotion Recognition (ERC)
Even though your primary signals are face and voice, the words being said are a third information source your own proposal architecture will eventually need (via ASR transcript) — these are the top ERC (Emotion Recognition in Conversation) approaches, which also solve a problem structurally identical to yours: segmenting a continuous conversation into emotion-labeled utterances.
MMGCN: Multimodal Fusion via Deep Graph Convolution Network for Emotion Recognition in Conversation
ACL 2021 (Association for Computational Linguistics)   |   Top-tier NLP conference (*CL)
Link: https://aclanthology.org/2021.acl-long.440/
Problem: Emotion in conversation depends on context (what was said/felt earlier in the dialogue) and on multiple speakers, not just the current isolated utterance.
Input / Output: Input: sequence of utterances, each with audio+visual+text features, across a multi-turn dialogue. Output: emotion label per utterance.
Methodology: Builds a graph where each utterance (per modality) is a node; edges capture intra-speaker temporal dependency and inter-modality dependency; graph convolution propagates context across the whole conversation. Evaluated on IEMOCAP and MELD.
Limitations / Gaps identified: Requires the full conversation graph to be built before inference — not naturally streaming/real-time, which conflicts with your live-camera, on-the-fly requirement; also assumes speaker-turn segmentation is already solved/given.
COGMEN: COntextualized GNN-based Multimodal Emotion RecognitioN
NAACL 2022 (North American ACL)   |   Top-tier NLP conference
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.02455
Problem: Extends MMGCN-style graph approaches by more explicitly separating 'local' context (immediate inter/intra-speaker dependency) from 'global' context (the whole conversation's arc).
Input / Output: Multimodal utterance sequence in; per-utterance emotion label out (IEMOCAP, MELD benchmarks).
Methodology: Graph Transformer architecture combining local relational-position encodings with global self-attention across the dialogue graph.
Limitations / Gaps identified: Same batch/offline-graph limitation as MMGCN; benchmark datasets (IEMOCAP is scripted, MELD is TV-show dialogue) are stylistically distant from a single user talking to a camera alone, which is your actual use case (monologue, not multi-party conversation) — an important structural gap.
MELD: A Multimodal Multi-Party Dataset for Emotion Recognition in Conversations
ACL 2019 (dataset paper underlying nearly all ERC work above)   |   Top-tier NLP conference — foundational dataset
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.02508
Problem: Prior emotion datasets (e.g., IEMOCAP) were either single/two-speaker or lab-recorded; MELD provides naturalistic multi-party TV-show dialogue with aligned audio, video, and text.
Input / Output: ~13,000 utterances from the show 'Friends', each with audio+video+transcript, labeled with 7 emotions and sentiment polarity.
Methodology: Dataset construction (source: TV show, splits, annotation protocol) — not a model.
Limitations / Gaps identified: Scripted/acted performance (professional actors), not spontaneous emotion; heavy dataset imbalance toward 'neutral'; used almost universally as the standard MER benchmark despite these acted-emotion caveats — worth explicitly flagging in your own limitations section if you use it.
5. Core Multimodal Fusion Architectures (Face + Voice [+ Text])
This is the section most directly reusable as your project's core architecture — how to combine the three modalities once each has its own embedding.
Multimodal Transformer for Unaligned Multimodal Language Sequences (MulT)
ACL 2019 — still the most-cited baseline architecture in 2024–2025 MER papers   |   Top-tier NLP conference (*CL) — foundational architecture
Link: https://aclanthology.org/P19-1656/
Problem: Text, audio, and video signals are sampled at different rates and are not naturally time-aligned (a word doesn't map cleanly to one video frame or one audio frame) — most prior fusion methods needed pre-aligned, hand-synced data.
Input / Output: Input: three parallel time-series — language (word embeddings), visual (per-frame facial features), acoustic (per-frame audio features). Output: sentiment/emotion score or class.
Methodology: Introduces 'directional pairwise cross-modal attention': each modality attends to every other modality across all time steps without requiring pre-alignment, then a self-attention transformer fuses the results. Outperformed prior SOTA by 10–15% on CMU-MOSI, CMU-MOSEI, and IEMOCAP.
Limitations / Gaps identified: Quadratic attention cost across all modality-pairs and time steps — computationally expensive for long, continuous recordings (a concern for a live/continuous monitoring app rather than short clips); doesn't handle a modality being fully absent (e.g., camera off) gracefully.
MISA: Modality-Invariant and -Specific Representations for Multimodal Sentiment Analysis
ACM Multimedia (ACM MM) 2020   |   Top-tier multimedia conference
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.03545
Problem: Naively fusing modalities conflates information that is shared across modalities (e.g., general sentence-level sentiment) with information unique to one modality (e.g., a vocal tremor with no textual trace), which hurts the fusion model's ability to learn either well.
Input / Output: Text + audio + visual features in; sentiment/emotion prediction out (CMU-MOSI/MOSEI).
Methodology: Projects each modality into two learned subspaces — a modality-invariant (shared) space and a modality-specific (private) space — using similarity and difference losses, then fuses both for final prediction.
Limitations / Gaps identified: The invariant/specific split is a soft, learned separation, not guaranteed to be semantically clean; still evaluated on short, single-utterance clips rather than continuous multi-minute sessions.
WavFusion: Towards Wav2vec 2.0 Multimodal Speech Emotion Recognition
arXiv 2024 (submitted to a speech-processing venue)   |   Recent (2024), strong empirical results
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.05558
Problem: Most multimodal SER fusion methods treat audio and its paired text as equally reliable, but they carry heterogeneous and sometimes conflicting emotional evidence (e.g., sarcasm: positive words, negative tone).
Input / Output: Speech (via wav2vec 2.0 embeddings) + text in; emotion class out (IEMOCAP, MELD).
Methodology: Gated cross-modal attention plus an explicit 'homogeneous feature discrepancy learning' objective that pushes the model to notice and reconcile disagreement between modalities rather than just averaging them.
Limitations / Gaps identified: Bimodal only (audio+text — no face/video); the discrepancy-learning objective adds training complexity/hyperparameters that could be brittle outside the two benchmark datasets tested.
SUN Team's Contribution to ABAW 2024: Audio-Visual Valence-Arousal Estimation and Expression Recognition
CVPR Workshops (CVPRW) 2024 — ABAW Challenge   |   Top-tier CV conference workshop / competitive challenge
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12609
Problem: Standard lab-trained audio-visual emotion models perform well on clean data but degrade sharply on 'in-the-wild' footage — exactly the ABAW challenge's stated goal (ecological validity).
Input / Output: Video (face) + audio in; either continuous valence-arousal scores or discrete expression class out, evaluated on the AffWild2 in-the-wild dataset.
Methodology: Separately fine-tunes a CNN for the video stream and a speech embedding model (PDEM) for audio, then compares several temporal-modeling and fusion strategies (early vs. late, attention-based) on top of these embeddings.
Limitations / Gaps identified: Explicitly reports that despite best efforts, in-the-wild performance remains well below lab-controlled benchmarks — direct evidence that your project's own 'robustness under real-world conditions' objective (lighting, noise, natural speaking style) is the field's known hardest open problem, not a solved one.
AV-Mixup / CH-SIMS v2.0: Make Acoustic and Visual Cues Matter
ACM International Conference on Multimodal Interaction (ICMI) 2022   |   Top-tier multimodal-interaction conference
Link: https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3536221.3556630
Problem: In many multimodal sentiment datasets, text dominates the prediction and audio/visual cues are effectively ignored by the model (a 'modality laziness' problem) even when they should matter.
Input / Output: Text + audio + visual features in; sentiment intensity/emotion out (CH-SIMS v2.0 dataset, Mandarin but methodology is language-agnostic).
Methodology: An AV-Mixup data augmentation technique that mixes acoustic/visual features across training samples specifically to force the model to rely on them, combined with a consistency-regularization module.
Limitations / Gaps identified: Confirms empirically that naive multimodal training can silently degrade into unimodal (text-dominant) behavior — a critical design warning for your fusion module: you must explicitly validate that facial and vocal signal are contributing, not just being nominally present in the architecture.
6. LLM-Based & Generalist Multimodal Emotion Understanding (2024–2025 Frontier)
The newest direction in the field (2024 onward) is using large multimodal models (GPT-4V-class or fine-tuned LLaMA-based models) not just to classify an emotion but to explain the reasoning — directly matching your stated requirement of '...and why' in the output.
Emotion-LLaMA: Multimodal Emotion Recognition and Reasoning with Instruction Tuning
NeurIPS 2024 (Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems)   |   Top-tier ML conference — one of the 3-4 most prestigious AI venues worldwide
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11161
Problem: Existing multimodal LLMs (e.g., GPT-4V) cannot process audio at all and fail to recognize subtle facial micro-expressions, making them unsuitable for genuine multimodal emotion reasoning out of the box.
Input / Output: Input: audio + video (visual) + text, via emotion-specific encoders. Output: not just an emotion label, but a natural-language explanation of why that emotion was inferred (emotion + reasoning).
Methodology: Introduces the MERR dataset (28,618 coarse-grained + 4,487 fine-grained emotion-reasoning annotated samples) and fine-tunes a modified LLaMA model with audio/visual encoders aligned into the LLM's token space via instruction tuning, in two training stages (coarse pretraining, then fine-grained instruction tuning).
Limitations / Gaps identified: Heavy compute/data requirements (large annotated reasoning dataset, LLM-scale fine-tuning) — likely infeasible for a student/early-stage project to fully reproduce, but its output format (label + explanation) is exactly the target UX your proposal describes and is worth adopting as a design pattern even with a smaller custom classifier.
GPT-4V with Emotion: A Zero-Shot Benchmark for Generalized Emotion Recognition
Information Fusion, Vol. 108, 2024 (Elsevier) — also circulated as arXiv 2312.04293   |   Q1 Journal
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04293
Problem: First systematic, quantitative test of whether a general-purpose multimodal LLM (GPT-4V), with zero task-specific training, can do emotion recognition competitively against specialized models.
Input / Output: Images/video/text across 21 benchmark datasets and 6 sub-tasks (facial emotion, dynamic facial emotion, micro-expression, visual sentiment, tweet sentiment, multimodal emotion) in; emotion prediction out.
Methodology: Prompts GPT-4V directly (no fine-tuning) across all 21 datasets and compares against published specialized-model baselines.
Limitations / Gaps identified: GPT-4V shows strong general visual understanding and can meaningfully integrate multimodal cues and even temporal information, but explicitly and reproducibly fails at micro-expression recognition (too subtle/specialized) and cannot process audio directly at all — a clear signal that a bespoke trained model (as your proposal plans) is still necessary rather than just wrapping a general API.
AffectGPT: Dataset and Framework for Explainable Multimodal Emotion Recognition
arXiv 2024 / ICML-affiliated workshop lineage (same author group as MER2024 challenge)   |   Actively cited emerging line of work
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.07653
Problem: Even reasoning-capable models like Emotion-LLaMA need a standardized dataset/framework to make 'explainable' emotion recognition comparable across research groups.
Input / Output: Audio+visual+text in; emotion label plus free-text explanation out.
Methodology: Proposes both a new dataset and a framework standardizing how explanation quality is evaluated (not just label accuracy).
Limitations / Gaps identified: Evaluating the quality of a generated natural-language explanation is inherently harder and less standardized than evaluating a classification accuracy number — the paper itself flags this measurement problem as unresolved, relevant if your own system needs to justify 'why' an emotion was detected.
7. Domain-Specific: Mental Health, Depression & Distress Detection
This is the section closest to your actual product. It uses the same facial+voice(+text) inputs as general emotion recognition, but targets a clinical/mental-health outcome instead of a basic emotion label — worth treating as your most important reference cluster.
Harnessing Multimodal Approaches for Depression Detection Using Large Language Models and Facial Expressions
npj Mental Health Research (Nature Portfolio), 2024   |   Q1 Journal — Nature-family journal
Link: https://www.nature.com/articles/s44184-024-00112-8
Problem: Depression screening traditionally relies on subjective self-report questionnaires (e.g., PHQ-8); the paper investigates whether facial expression + LLM-processed interview transcript can approximate/support that screening automatically.
Input / Output: Input: video (facial expressions) + interview transcript, from the DAIC-WOZ / E-DAIC clinical interview dataset (participant talks to a virtual/human-controlled interviewer 'Ellie'). Output: depression risk / PHQ-8-aligned severity indicator.
Methodology: Combines facial-expression feature extraction with an LLM-based analysis of the interview transcript; compares against unimodal baselines.
Limitations / Gaps identified: Explicitly a research/screening-support tool, not a diagnostic one; DAIC-WOZ is a structured clinical interview (fixed question set, calm interviewer), which is a much more controlled setting than a user freely talking to a webcam at home — an important domain-shift caveat for your app.
Bidirectional Cross-Modal Attention with Adaptive Thresholding for Multimodal Depression Detection Across EATD-Corpus and DAIC-WOZ
BioMedInformatics (MDPI), 2025   |   Q2 Journal
Link: https://doi.org/10.3390/biomedinformatics6050070
Problem: Depression datasets are small and heavily class-imbalanced (far fewer depressed than non-depressed samples), which causes models to overfit or trivially predict the majority class.
Input / Output: Audio + text in (two corpora: Chinese EATD-Corpus, English DAIC-WOZ); binary depressed/non-depressed classification out, with F1/recall/precision reported.
Methodology: Bidirectional cross-modal attention between audio and text, SMOTE-based oversampling for class imbalance, plus adaptive decision thresholding and probability ensembling to stabilize predictions.
Limitations / Gaps identified: No visual/facial modality at all in this particular paper (audio+text only); cross-corpus generalization (a model trained on one language/corpus applied to the other) is explicitly tested and shown to be imperfect, underscoring that a model trained on public datasets will need real recalibration on your own users' data.
IMDD-Net: Integrative Multimodal Depression Detection Network (video-audio-text)
PMC/peer-reviewed journal, 2024–2025 (open access)   |   Peer-reviewed journal
Link: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12504862/
Problem: Existing depression-detection networks rarely combine local (frame/segment-level) and global (session-level) features across all three modalities simultaneously.
Input / Output: Video + audio + text in (all three modalities, matching your proposal directly); depression severity estimate out.
Methodology: Kronecker-product-based multimodal fusion to model deep cross-modal interactions; MFCC + eGeMAPS acoustic features (local + global statistics) combined with periodic video-frame sampling.
Limitations / Gaps identified: Kronecker-product fusion has a dimensionality explosion problem as more modalities/features are added, raising compute cost; like other DAIC-WOZ-based work, trained on structured clinical interviews rather than spontaneous unscripted speech.
Multi-Modal LLM for Depression Detection by Integrating Text, Audio, and Video
arXiv 2025 (submitted to an ML/health-informatics venue)   |   Recent (2025), state-of-the-art on DAIC-WoZ
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.19877
Problem: First reported attempt (per the authors) at a true tri-modal (text+audio+video) LLM-based depression detector, versus prior work that was mostly bimodal (text+audio only).
Input / Output: Text + audio + video in; depression classification out, on DAIC-WoZ.
Methodology: Built on Qwen2-Audio (an audio-native LLM) augmented with a self-supervised vision encoder and parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA-style), rather than training a bespoke classifier from scratch.
Limitations / Gaps identified: The authors themselves explicitly disclaim the model for research use only — not for clinical diagnosis, treatment, or intervention — and note the general LLM risks of hallucination and bias, a disclaimer your own project should adopt verbatim in its own scope statement given it targets 'mental health monitoring.'
DAIC-WOZ / E-DAIC: Distress Analysis Interview Corpus
Originating from AVEC (Audio/Visual Emotion Challenge) series; USC ICT   |   Foundational clinical dataset — used across nearly all papers in this section
Link: https://dcapswoz.ict.usc.edu/
What it is: The standard public benchmark for multimodal (audio, video, text-transcript) depression/anxiety/PTSD screening: interviews between real participants and 'Ellie,' a human-controlled virtual agent, labeled with PHQ-8 depression severity scores.
Why it matters to you: It is the closest existing public dataset, in structure, to what your app will collect (a person talking to a camera/mic, with derivable text via ASR) — strongly recommended as your first external benchmark before collecting your own 'custom dataset for real-world evaluation' as stated in your proposal.
Limitations / Gaps identified: Only ~189 (DAIC-WOZ) to 275 (E-DAIC) participants — small by deep-learning standards, and heavily imbalanced toward non-depressed; a fixed, calm interview format rather than free unscripted speech; several recent papers (see IMDD-Net entry) note possible label leakage/shortcut learning risk (e.g., models learning to detect an interviewer's follow-up-question pattern rather than genuine affective cues) — worth being aware of before you build evaluation protocols around it.

9. Requirement Analysis Takeaways
9.1 Functional Requirements Implied by the Literature
Face detection + alignment preprocessing stage is a prerequisite for every FER paper reviewed (POSTER, POSTER++, DFER Transformer) — your pipeline needs this as an explicit, separate module, not an assumption baked into the classifier.
A voice-activity-detection / utterance-segmentation module is needed before per-utterance classification, since no reviewed model performs this from raw continuous audio+video end-to-end.
An explicit 'modality contribution check' (ablation-style, at inference time or as a periodic validation) is warranted given documented 'modality laziness' risk in fusion training.
Cross-corpus / cross-condition evaluation should be built into your test plan from the start (train on public datasets — FER2013/AffectNet/RAVDESS/CREMA-D as you propose — but evaluate on your own collected 'in-the-wild' data early, not only at the end), since every survey reviewed flags this generalization gap as the field's central unsolved problem.
Output should include a confidence/probability distribution across emotions rather than a single hard label, both because your proposal already plans this and because the AffectNet+ paper shows single hard labels lose real information for genuinely ambiguous/compound expressions.
9.2 Non-Functional Requirements Implied by the Literature
Real-time performance is achievable but requires deliberate architecture choices: the edge-deployment papers reviewed (Section 5/8) show 21ms–200ms latency is possible via quantization, knowledge distillation, and lightweight backbones (ShuffleNet, DistilHuBERT/MobileViT-class models) — full Transformer-scale fusion models (MulT-style) are unlikely to hit real-time targets without these techniques.
Privacy: on-device/federated approaches exist as concrete, citable precedent (not just a policy statement) if your later product roadmap needs to process camera/mic data locally rather than sending it to a server.
Robustness to missing modality (camera off, mic muted, or poor lighting/noisy environment) should be a designed-for failure mode, not an edge case discovered late — several fusion papers (MISA, CH-SIMS v2.0/AV-Mixup) exist specifically because naive fusion breaks when one modality's signal quality drops.
Ethical/scope framing: every mental-health-adjacent paper reviewed (DAIC-WOZ-based depression detection, Multi-Modal LLM for Depression Detection) explicitly and prominently disclaims clinical/diagnostic use — your project should adopt equivalent language from the outset given your application's stated 'mental health monitoring' framing, to stay aligned with how this exact research community frames responsible deployment.

11. Suggested Reading Order & Code Repositories
For a project at requirement-analysis stage, this is a practical order to read/implement in:
1. Read Section 1 surveys fully — they will anchor your literature review chapter and give you citation-ready taxonomy language.
2. Skim Section 2 (FER) and Section 3 (SER) entries to pick your two unimodal backbones — POSTER++ (or a lighter ViT-FER model) for face, fine-tuned wav2vec2/HuBERT for voice.

5. Treat Emotion-LLaMA / GPT-4V-with-Emotion (Section 6) as aspirational/future-work reference points (per your proposal's own 'Future Extensions' section), not as your MVP target.
