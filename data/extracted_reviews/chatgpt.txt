Multimodal Emotion Recognition for a Real-Time Mental-Health Monitoring Application
Literature Review, Research Gap Analysis, and System Requirement Mapping
Executive finding. The proposed system is not merely a facial-expression classifier or speech-emotion classifier. The closest research problem is multimodal emotion recognition (MER), especially multimodal emotion recognition in conversations (MERC), where text, acoustic/prosodic and visual signals are fused under temporal and speaker context. The literature shows that the strongest research opportunities for your project are not 'add three models and concatenate them'; they are asynchronous alignment, adaptive fusion, missing/noisy modality handling, calibrated confidence, continuous temporal modeling, and psychologically defensible explanations.
Project baseline. The supplied project proposal defines facial video + voice as inputs, facial analysis, MFCC/pitch/spectral speech features, CNN/ViT and LSTM/Transformer families, multimodal fusion, confidence/trend visualization, and evaluation under lighting/noise/speaking-style variation. It also names FER2013, AffectNet, RAVDESS and CREMA-D as candidate datasets.
1. Scope and terminology
This review uses MER for multimodal emotion recognition and MERC when the task is explicitly conversational/utterance-level. A 'trimodal' system here means text + audio + visual; an audiovisual system is audio + visual without text. Your intended live camera-and-microphone experience is closest to trimodal MERC, but it also adds a streaming segmentation problem that benchmark papers usually simplify by giving pre-segmented utterances.
Venue status is separated into conference tier and journal quartile because Q1/Q2/Q3 is primarily a journal-ranking concept. The ICORE 2026 portal lists AAAI, ACL and ACM Multimedia as A* conferences; the report therefore uses conference-tier language instead of forcing a Q1 label onto conferences.

4. Detailed reading notes: top 12 papers to read first
1. M3ER: Multiplicative Multimodal Emotion Recognition using Facial, Textual, and Speech Cues
Mittal et al. (2020) — AAAI 2020. Top conference (AAAI; ICORE 2026 A*)
Research issue: How to fuse co-occurring multimodal emotion cues while remaining robust when one modality is noisy or ineffective.
Input: Utterance-level facial, textual and speech features from IEMOCAP and CMU-MOSEI.
Output: Emotion classification with a fused prediction.
Methodology: Data-driven multiplicative fusion; a CCA-based modality check identifies ineffective modalities; proxy features replace unreliable modalities.
Limitations / gap: Benchmark-driven and largely utterance-level; the proxy-modality mechanism is not the same as true missing-modality uncertainty. Does not solve continuous live segmentation, personalization, or clinical validity.
Links: Paper | arXiv
2. MELD: A Multimodal Multi-Party Dataset for Emotion Recognition in Conversations
Poria et al. (2019) — ACL 2019. Top conference (ACL; ICORE 2026 A*)
Research issue: Provide a realistic multimodal, multi-party conversational benchmark for utterance-level emotion recognition.
Input: ~13k utterances in 1,433 Friends dialogues; aligned text/audio/visual streams.
Output: Seven emotion labels plus sentiment labels for each utterance.
Methodology: Dataset creation/annotation plus strong baselines; models can exploit textual, acoustic, visual and dialogue-context information.
Limitations / gap: Acted TV dialogue is not equivalent to spontaneous private mental-health conversations; class imbalance is substantial; speaker/context distribution is narrow; visual/audio reliability can be imperfect.
Links: Paper | GitHub
3. Multimodal Language Analysis in the Wild: CMU-MOSEI Dataset and Interpretable Dynamic Fusion Graph
Zadeh et al. (2018) — ACL 2018. Top conference (ACL; ICORE 2026 A*)
Research issue: Create a large-scale in-the-wild multimodal benchmark and model interactions among asynchronous modalities.
Input: Asynchronous words, facial/visual features and acoustic features from online videos.
Output: Sentiment and emotion-intensity predictions.
Methodology: CMU-MOSEI dataset plus Dynamic Fusion Graph (DFG), designed to model modality interactions while preserving interpretability.
Limitations / gap: Centered on multimodal language/sentiment-style tasks rather than a clinical emotion-monitoring setting; labels are not a direct measure of mental-health state; feature extraction and segmentation assumptions matter.
Links: Paper | ACL 2018
4. MulT: Multimodal Transformer for Unaligned Multimodal Language Sequences
Tsai et al. (2019) — ACL 2019. Top conference (ACL; ICORE 2026 A*)
Research issue: Handle asynchronous sampling rates and long-range cross-modal dependencies without explicit hard alignment.
Input: Unaligned multimodal time series.
Output: Task prediction from fused multimodal representation; widely reused for sentiment/emotion.
Methodology: Directional pairwise cross-modal attention; each target modality attends to source modality sequences, followed by temporal/self-attention fusion.
Limitations / gap: Fusion is powerful but computationally expensive; it is not inherently a live-stream segmentation solution and does not itself solve confidence calibration or modality reliability.
Links: Paper | GitHub
5. Self-attention fusion for audiovisual emotion recognition with incomplete data
Chumachenko, Iosifidis & Gabbouj (2022) — ICPR 2022. Major pattern-recognition conference
Research issue: Recognize emotion when one audiovisual modality is absent or noisy.
Input: Audio/video emotion clips with intentionally missing/noisy modalities.
Output: Emotion label.
Methodology: Raw-data audiovisual encoders with alternative fusion mechanisms plus modality dropout during training.
Limitations / gap: Bimodal rather than tri-modal; incomplete-modality training does not fully address semantic contradictions among text, audio and face; dataset conditions are still controlled relative to a consumer application.
Links: Paper | GitHub
6. M2FNet: Multi-modal Fusion Network for Emotion Recognition in Conversation
Chudasama et al. (2022) — CVPR Workshops 2022. Major computer-vision workshop
Research issue: Exploit all three conversational modalities instead of relying mainly on text.
Input: Conversational transcript, audio and face/video streams.
Output: Utterance emotion; reported performance on MELD and IEMOCAP.
Methodology: Multi-head attention-based fusion; audio/visual feature extractor trained with adaptive-margin triplet loss to produce emotion-relevant representations.
Limitations / gap: Workshop venue; relies on benchmark feature pipelines; does not address true mental-health validation or open-vocabulary/fine-grained explanation.
Links: Paper | IEEE
7. Noise-Resistant Multimodal Transformer for Emotion Recognition
Liu et al. (2025) — International Journal of Computer Vision (IJCV), 133:3020-3040. Top journal; IJCV is Q1
Research issue: Noisy multimodal sequences contain semantically useless regions that can dominate fusion.
Input: Multimodal video/text/audio sequences with noisy segments.
Output: Emotion recognition.
Methodology: Noise-resistant multimodal Transformer that explicitly suppresses irrelevant/noisy temporal information and improves multimodal representation quality.
Limitations / gap: Still benchmark-oriented; the paper tackles noise but not the broader uncertainty problem of conflicting modalities, individual baselines, or clinical interpretation.
Links: Paper metadata | DBLP
8. Fusing pairwise modalities for emotion recognition in conversations
Fan, Lin, Mao & Cambria (2024) — Information Fusion 106, 102306. Q1 journal (SCImago 2025)
Research issue: Complex inter-modal relationships and redundancy make naive all-at-once fusion brittle.
Input: MERC conversational modalities.
Output: Conversation emotion classification.
Methodology: Pairwise modality management plus a density loss to reduce redundant representations and improve robustness.
Limitations / gap: Pairwise fusion can miss higher-order interactions among all three modalities; it does not directly solve temporal streaming, explanation, personalization, or mental-health outcome validation.
Links: Paper
9. Multimodal Emotion Recognition Calibration in Conversations
Tu et al. (2024) — ACM Multimedia 2024. Top conference (ACM MM; ICORE 2026 A*)
Research issue: A classifier can be accurate yet confidently wrong, especially after modality/context corruption.
Input: MERC utterances with multimodal corruption/uncertainty cases.
Output: Emotion label plus better-calibrated confidence.
Methodology: CMERC: curriculum learning, hybrid supervised contrastive learning, and confidence constraints to calibrate predictive confidence without changing the backbone architecture.
Limitations / gap: Calibration is necessary but not equivalent to trustworthy explanation; confidence is still model confidence, not a clinical probability of an underlying mental state.
Links: Paper PDF | GitHub
10. Emotion-LLaMA: Multimodal Emotion Recognition and Reasoning with Instruction Tuning
Cheng et al. (2024) — arXiv 2024; reported as a modern multimodal emotion-reasoning line. Recent foundation-model direction
Research issue: Traditional classifiers recognize labels but struggle to integrate audio and subtle facial cues or explain their decisions.
Input: Multimodal samples including audio, visual content and text/captions.
Output: Emotion labels and natural-language emotion reasoning.
Methodology: Emotion-specific encoders align audio/visual/text into a shared space, then a LLaMA-based model is instruction-tuned for emotion recognition and reasoning; introduces MERR and evaluates MER tasks.
Limitations / gap: Large-model cost/latency; reasoning may be plausible rather than faithful; dataset mixture and generated explanations can create evaluation leakage; clinical validity remains unaddressed.
Links: Paper | GitHub
11. MERBench: A Unified Evaluation Benchmark for Multimodal Emotion Recognition
Lian et al. (2024) — arXiv / benchmark. Benchmark paper / infrastructure
Research issue: Published MER methods often use different features, splits, evaluation settings and pretraining choices, making comparisons unfair.
Input: Standardized multimodal benchmark inputs.
Output: Comparable benchmark metrics and analysis.
Methodology: Unified benchmark protocol covering feature extraction, fusion, robustness, fine-tuning and pretraining; introduces MER2023 and an open toolkit.
Limitations / gap: Benchmark unification cannot solve dataset validity; MER2023 is Chinese, whereas your target is English; models can still overfit benchmark conventions.
Links: Paper | Code | Dataset
12. EMOE: Modality-Specific Enhanced Dynamic Emotion Experts
Fang et al. (2025) — CVPR 2025. Top conference (CVPR)
Research issue: Different samples need different modality weights; fusion can erase modality-specific predictive power.
Input: Multimodal emotion inputs.
Output: Emotion prediction.
Methodology: Mixture of Modality Experts to dynamically weight modalities per sample, plus unimodal distillation to preserve modality-specific knowledge.
Limitations / gap: Dynamic weights are useful but not automatically interpretable; benchmark data may not reflect consumer camera/microphone quality or spontaneous behavior.
Links: Paper
5. Supporting papers and building blocks
DialogueRNN: An Attentive RNN for Emotion Detection in Conversations — AAAI 2019. Speaker-specific recurrent states; essential for temporal/speaker context, but primarily conversational rather than full tri-modal fusion.
DialogueGCN: A Graph Convolutional Neural Network for Emotion Recognition in Conversation — EMNLP-IJCNLP 2019. Graph context with self/inter-speaker dependencies; useful for modeling evolving conversational state.
COSMIC: COmmonSense knowledge for eMotion Identification in Conversations — Findings EMNLP 2020. Adds commonsense, mental state, event and causal relations; useful when words alone are ambiguous.
DialogueCRN: Contextual Reasoning Networks for Emotion Recognition in Conversations — ACL 2021. Iterative contextual reasoning for emotion clues; relevant to your 'why' explanation goal but not a full multimodal live system.
Learning Modality-Specific Representations with Self-Supervised Multi-Task Learning for Multimodal Sentiment Analysis — AAAI 2021. Separates shared and modality-specific information with generated unimodal supervision; useful for preserving voice/face-specific cues.
Improving Multimodal Fusion with Hierarchical Mutual Information Maximization for Multimodal Sentiment Analysis — EMNLP 2021. Uses mutual-information maximization at two levels to improve cross-modal fusion.
UniMSE: Towards Unified Multimodal Sentiment Analysis and Emotion Recognition — EMNLP 2022. Unifies multimodal sentiment and emotion using shared knowledge, syntactic/semantic fusion and contrastive learning.
Emotion-Anchored Contrastive Learning Framework for Emotion Recognition in Conversation — Findings NAACL 2024. Separates similar emotions using label anchors and contrastive learning; useful for happiness/excitement-type confusion.
DFEW: A Large-Scale Database for Recognizing Dynamic Facial Expressions in the Wild — ACM MM 2020. Large-scale dynamic face benchmark with real-world pose, occlusion and illumination variation.
MAFW: A Large-scale, Multi-modal, Compound Affective Database for Dynamic Facial Expression Recognition in the Wild — ACM MM 2022. 10,045 video-audio clips; supports richer compound affect labels and multimodal dynamic face analysis.
The Ryerson Audio-Visual Database of Emotional Speech and Song (RAVDESS) — PLOS ONE 2018. Validated North American English audiovisual emotion dataset with face-and-voice, face-only and voice-only versions.
Emotional Expressions Reconsidered: Challenges to Inferring Emotion From Human Facial Movements — Psychological Science in the Public Interest 2019. Critical psychological grounding: facial movements have limited reliability, specificity and generalizability for inferring discrete emotions.

7. The strongest research gaps for this exact project
Gap 1 — Live segmentation and alignment
Most benchmark papers receive pre-segmented utterances. Your app must infer when one emotional unit ends and another begins, while video/audio/text are sampled at different rates. This is a core streaming problem, not a minor preprocessing detail.
Gap 2 — Contradictory modalities
A person may say 'I'm fine' while sounding flat and showing sadness, or smile while discussing something distressing. Existing work often asks which modality helps prediction, but a practical system must explicitly represent disagreement rather than force one fused label.
Gap 3 — Expression is not equivalent to inner state
A facial movement can be socially strategic, ambiguous, culturally variable, or unrelated to emotion. The psychological literature specifically identifies limited reliability, specificity and generalizability in mapping facial movements to discrete emotions. This is especially important when the product framing mentions mental health.
Gap 4 — Calibration and uncertainty
Your proposal includes a confidence score. CMERC shows why raw softmax confidence is not enough: corruption can produce increased confidence. A useful system should estimate uncertainty and indicate when evidence is insufficient or contradictory.
Gap 5 — Continuous state vs. utterance label
MERC benchmarks mostly predict an emotion for each utterance. Your dashboard wants trends over time. That introduces smoothing, temporal hysteresis, change-point detection, baseline personalization and aggregation over minutes/hours/days.
Gap 6 — Personalization
Population-trained models assume that expressive behavior maps similarly across people. Your application would benefit from a lightweight personal baseline: typical speaking rate, pitch range, facial activity and neutral expression, without treating deviations as pathology.
Gap 7 — Explanations are weakly grounded
Attention weights, prototype similarities and LLM-generated rationales are not automatically causal explanations. A robust 'why' should report evidence by modality, time range and uncertainty (e.g., 'vocal pitch increased during this phrase; facial AU pattern supported sadness; words were neutral').
Gap 8 — Mental-health construct validity
Emotion recognition and mental-health monitoring are different prediction targets. A model can recognize 'sadness' accurately without demonstrating that it can detect depression, anxiety or distress severity. A project should therefore frame the first product as affect/emotion monitoring, not diagnosis.
Gap 9 — Real-world domain shift
Lighting, camera angle, microphone quality, background noise, glasses, facial occlusion, accent, speaking style and spontaneous behavior can break benchmark assumptions. Domain-shift testing must be designed from the start.
Gap 10 — English is simpler but not simple
Restricting to English avoids multilingual representation issues, but speech emotion still varies with accent, dialect, culture, age, gender and code-switching. English-only should be treated as a scope boundary, not as a guarantee of uniformity.
8. Requirements analysis for the proposed system
9. Evidence-backed architecture direction
The literature suggests a research architecture with separate modality encoders, a shared time axis, adaptive cross-modal interaction and a context/trend layer. A reasonable starting hypothesis is:
Camera -> face detection/tracking -> quality estimation -> temporal visual encoder (ViT/3D-CNN/face-AU or learned facial tokens)
Microphone -> speech activity detection -> SSL speech encoder + prosody features (pitch/F0, energy, rate, pauses, spectral descriptors)
Audio -> ASR -> word/subword timestamps -> language encoder (e.g., BERT/RoBERTa-family or a compact modern encoder)
Time alignment -> word/audio/video windows anchored on utterance or phrase segments
Adaptive fusion -> cross-modal attention or dynamic mixture-of-experts; add modality dropout/noise simulation during training
Context layer -> short-term temporal Transformer or graph/context model with speaker history
Prediction layer -> categorical emotion + optional valence/arousal + calibrated confidence + disagreement/insufficient-evidence flag
Explanation layer -> modality evidence summaries grounded in observed time windows, not free-form unsupported reasoning
Trend layer -> confidence-weighted smoothing and longitudinal aggregation
This is a research direction, not a claim that one cited paper already solves the full pipeline. The literature supports each component separately; integrating all of them into a low-latency, uncertainty-aware, personalized English streaming system is where the project can become a meaningful systems contribution.
10. What should count as the research contribution?
A streaming trimodal emotion model that performs alignment and segmentation online rather than relying on pre-segmented benchmark utterances.
An adaptive fusion mechanism that can explicitly down-weight unreliable or contradictory face/audio/text evidence.
A calibrated emotion output with an 'insufficient evidence' state, rather than always returning a confident class.
A grounded explanation format that reports what each modality contributed and where in time, instead of treating LLM-generated prose as proof.
A realistic cross-dataset and cross-person evaluation protocol: train on public datasets, test across held-out speakers/datasets, then evaluate a small spontaneous real-world set under controlled consent.
A longitudinal trend layer that turns per-utterance emotion into a stable monitoring signal without equating emotion trends with a psychiatric diagnosis.

1) MELD — understand the tri-modal conversational benchmark and its assumptions.
2) M3ER — understand direct face/text/speech fusion and modality reliability.
3) MulT — understand asynchronous cross-modal attention.
4) M2FNet — inspect a tri-modal conversational architecture close to the proposed application.
5) Noise-Resistant Multimodal Transformer — understand robustness to noisy temporal regions.
6) GCNet + modality-dropout work — understand incomplete/missing modality scenarios.
7) CMERC — understand why a confidence score needs calibration.
8) EMOE + pairwise fusion — understand adaptive modality weighting and interaction.
9) Emotion-LLaMA + VEGA — understand the current 'recognize + reason + ground' direction.
10) Barrett et al. — use this to keep the mental-health framing scientifically defensible.
13. Practical recommendation for the project proposal
The proposal should be reframed slightly from 'detect a person's mental health from face and voice' toward 'estimate momentary emotional state from synchronized facial, vocal and linguistic cues, with uncertainty and longitudinal trend analysis.' That wording matches the actual research field and reduces the risk of implying clinical diagnosis. Your current proposal already identifies privacy, real-world robustness, facial/voice fusion, confidence scores and future behavioral/physiological extensions; these are strong anchors for the literature-backed version of the project.
The most defensible novelty is not inventing yet another CNN + LSTM fusion block. A stronger undergraduate/system-design contribution would be a well-engineered, reproducible streaming pipeline with adaptive fusion, missing-modality robustness, calibration, and a grounded explanation protocol, evaluated across multiple English datasets and real-world perturbations.

ID | Requirement area | Interpretation
R1 | Multimodal capture | Camera video + microphone audio + ASR transcript
R2 | Asynchronous alignment | Map words, audio frames and face frames onto a common time axis
R3 | Utterance segmentation | Detect sentence/phrase boundaries using pause duration, ASR segmentation and conversational turn cues
R4 | Facial dynamics | Track face identity/quality, expression/action-unit or learned visual tokens, not a single still frame
R5 | Speech emotion | Use both SSL speech representation and interpretable prosodic features such as F0/pitch, energy, rate and pauses
R6 | Text emotion | Use contextual language representation; preserve the exact words for semantic evidence
R7 | Adaptive fusion | Learn sample-specific modality contributions and model disagreement
R8 | Missing/noisy modality handling | Continue sensibly when video/audio quality drops
R9 | Temporal context | Use recent utterances and speaker-specific history, not only the current sentence
R10 | Uncertainty/calibration | Return calibrated confidence and an abstain/insufficient-evidence state
R11 | Explainability | Return modality-specific evidence spans and an overall reason, clearly marked as model inference
R12 | Trend layer | Aggregate utterance predictions into a time-series representation with smoothing and uncertainty
R13 | Privacy | Prefer on-device or edge processing where practical; avoid retaining raw face/audio unless explicitly needed
R14 | Evaluation | Report macro-F1/UAR/WAR as appropriate, confusion matrices, calibration metrics, latency, missing-modality robustness and cross-person generalization
