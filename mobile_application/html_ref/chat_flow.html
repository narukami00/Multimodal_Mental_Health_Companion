03

Design rationale

How the screen honors the non-negotiables.

Single focus, protected

The tab bar disappears; the header shrinks to back + presence + two peer icons; the input bar carries exactly three controls with mic and send sharing one slot. On any screen there is only ever one amber thing to press. The end-session row is a whisper in the corner, present but never competing.

Non-punitive by construction

No streaks, no scores, no "you missed a day." The ended state thanks the user in human language and quotes their words back — proof they were heard, not measured. Every chip set includes "I'd rather not say" as a first-class, unembarrassed option; the sheet dismissals say "Maybe later" and "I'm okay for now."

Human-readable first

The only numbers anywhere are a clock time per cluster and a voice-note duration. No emotion labels, no confidence, no analytics. Timestamps are grouped so the transcript reads like a conversation, not a log file. The Journal card says "Monday evening," never "14 messages · 82% sentiment."

Trust-first optionality

Voice, camera, call, and attachments are peers: identical tile weights, no badges, no "new" flags, no empty-state nagging. Recording can always be cancelled without sending, and cancelling costs nothing. The crisis heart sits in the same pixel in every state, and opening it is private — Sol never comments on it in-thread.

One companion, two modes

The same breathing soft-shape appears at three scales — full-size greeting (echoing the call avatar), 24dp header presence, and 26dp thinking pulse — so text and voice read as one being, not two products. Shared gradient, shared coral "end" semantics, shared cream surfaces and Nunito. The header call icon is a door between modes, not an upsell: same weight as the heart beside it.

Friction budget for hard days

A tired or mid-crisis user makes ~zero extra decisions: chips answer the blank-page problem; the send button appears only when needed (no dead-state scanning); recording morphs the existing pill instead of launching a new screen; the keyboard never jumps. Everything destructive is placed far from everything frequent.

04

Open questions & opportunities

Flagged for the next design crit and the deferred accessibility pass.

1
Emotion-reactive avatar motion
Carrying over the call-screen flag: should the header blob's tempo/warmth shift with conversation tone (slower & softer amber in distress, brighter when light)? Opportunity for deep companionship — risk of feeling surveilled. Proposal: subtle-only, and make it discoverable/opt-out in You tab. Needs user testing before build.
2
Proactive crisis support — without interruptive modals
MVP keeps the support sheet user-opened only. If risk language is detected, could Sol offer support in-thread as a warm message + a quiet inline card (same coral-tint language) rather than a blocking dialog? Defining that escalation choreography is a follow-up project with clinical input.
3
Reply pacing & thinking-time honesty
Should the thinking indicator's duration mirror message length (a longer reply "takes a moment")? Instant replies can feel canned; artificial delay can feel dishonest. A/B the 1.2–2.4s band with real users in slump states.
4
Mode hand-off continuity
When the user taps Call mid-conversation, does Sol carry context into the voice room ("you were telling me about work…")? High warmth potential; needs a privacy framing ("Sol remembers this conversation") so it never startles.
5
Mood sticker vocabulary
If stickers ship, keep them abstract-warm (weather, blobs, textures — "foggy," "heavy," "a bit of sun") rather than emoji faces with labels, to stay clear of emotion-labeling the user. Commission a 6–8 piece set matching the blob gradient family.
6
Journal summary control
The ended card auto-saves. Should users preview/edit what's saved before it lands in the Journal? Trust argues yes; friction argues it must stay one tap. Prototype: a tappable card that expands into an editable snippet.
7
Night-mode warm palette
Many sessions happen late (the mockups are set at 9:41 PM). Cream #FEFCE8 is bright in a dark room. A warm-dark variant (deep cocoa #2A2118 surfaces, dimmed amber) is a fast-follow — must preserve contrast ratios and the blob's glow.
8
Deferred accessibility pass
TalkBack traversal order for bubbles + sheets, focus rings on amber controls, reduced-motion fallbacks for breathe/pulse/wave, and a contrast re-check of ink-faint meta text. 48dp targets and AA body contrast are already baked in.
